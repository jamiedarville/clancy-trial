# case-requirements.md

**Case:** Commonwealth of Massachusetts v. Lindsay Clancy
**Court:** Plymouth Superior Court, Plymouth, MA — Judge William F. Sullivan presiding
**Trial dates:** Jury selection began 2026-07-20; testimony concluded 2026-08-26; closings 2026-08-27; deliberations began 2026-08-27
**Real-world outcome as of 2026-09-04:** Jury deadlocked after seven days. Judge stated intent to declare a mistrial; defense filed emergency appeal to the SJC.

## 0. PROVENANCE WARNING — READ BEFORE USE

This file is **not** the trial record. It is assembled from contemporaneous news
reporting of the proceedings. It is subject to the following distortions, which
any agent consuming it must treat as live limitations:

- **Selection bias.** Reporters covered what was newsworthy. Routine and
  technical testimony (forensic pathology, phone-extraction methodology, chain of
  custody, most of 70+ prosecution witnesses) is almost entirely absent here.
- **Compression.** Three weeks of cross-examination is reduced to a handful of
  lines. Cross is where most real juror doubt is generated. It is missing.
- **No demeanor.** Real jurors weighed how witnesses and the defendant presented.
  That channel does not exist in this file.
- **No exhibits.** Photographs, the medication chart, phone-extraction reports,
  medical records, and the 911 audio were all in evidence and are not here.
- **Asymmetric coverage.** Expert conclusions were reported heavily; the
  foundations for those conclusions were not. This structurally favors whichever
  side's expert produced the more quotable conclusion.

**Do not treat absence of an item as absence of evidence.** Where a juror agent
would want a fact that is not present, it should say so explicitly and record it
in its reasoning rather than inventing it.

## 1. CHARGES AND LEGAL STANDARD

**C-001** Three counts of murder in the first degree, on theories of deliberate
premeditation and extreme atrocity or cruelty. Three counts of strangulation.

**C-002** Lesser included offenses available to the jury: murder in the second
degree, and manslaughter.

**C-003** The defendant pleaded not guilty and raised lack of criminal
responsibility (the Massachusetts formulation of the insanity defense).

**C-004 — CONTROLLING STANDARD (*Commonwealth v. McHoul*).** A defendant is not
criminally responsible if, as a result of mental disease or defect, she lacked
**substantial capacity** either (a) to appreciate the wrongfulness of her
conduct, **or** (b) to conform her conduct to the requirements of law.

**C-005 — BURDEN.** Once the issue of criminal responsibility is properly raised,
the Commonwealth bears the burden of proving criminal responsibility **beyond a
reasonable doubt**. The defendant does not have to prove insanity.

> **Note for the judge agent:** C-004 and C-005 are the most outcome-determinative
> content in this entire file — more than any evidentiary item. The disjunctive
> "either/or" in C-004 and the allocation of burden in C-005 must be delivered to
> juror agents verbatim and must not be paraphrased into a "did she know right
> from wrong" formulation, which is the *M'Naghten* standard and is **not** the
> law in Massachusetts.

**C-006** Verdict must be unanimous. Consequences of a verdict are not a proper
subject of jury deliberation. (Real-world consequences, for the experimenter's
reference only: guilty of murder 1 → life without parole; not guilty by reason of
lack of criminal responsibility → commitment to a state psychiatric facility
subject to periodic recommitment hearings.)

## 2. UNDISPUTED FACTS

**E-001** `[UNDISPUTED]` On 2023-01-24, at the family home on Summer Street,
Duxbury, MA, the defendant strangled her three children with exercise resistance
bands: Cora (5), Dawson (3), and Callan (8 months).

**E-002** `[UNDISPUTED]` Cora and Dawson were pronounced dead shortly after.
Callan survived for several days in hospital before dying.

**E-003** `[UNDISPUTED]` The defendant then attempted suicide, inflicting cuts to
her wrists and neck and jumping from a second-story window.

**E-004** `[UNDISPUTED]` The fall left her permanently paralyzed from the waist
down. She has been in custody at a state hospital since.

**E-005** `[UNDISPUTED]` The defendant was a labor and delivery nurse. She had no
prior history of violence and no criminal record.

**E-006** `[UNDISPUTED]` The defendant had a documented, treated psychiatric
illness in the months preceding the killings.

**E-007** `[UNDISPUTED]` The defendant never spoke to law enforcement; she was
hospitalized for her injuries throughout the investigative period. There is no
police interview.

**E-008** `[UNDISPUTED]` The defendant did not testify at trial.

## 3. BACKGROUND TIMELINE (Sept 2022 – Jan 2023)

**E-010** `[DOCUMENT]` Callan, the third child, was born in mid-2022. The
defendant's mental health deteriorated significantly beginning in the fall of
2022.

**E-011** `[DOCUMENT]` Between September 2022 and January 2023 the defendant was
prescribed 13 different psychiatric medications by multiple providers —
psychiatrists, nurse practitioners, emergency department physicians, and inpatient
program staff. Reporting of court records indicates more than 30 individual
prescriptions were issued across that four-month period.

**E-012** `[DOCUMENT]` The medications spanned several classes: SSRIs, atypical
antipsychotics, benzodiazepines, a mood stabilizer, and sedatives.

**E-013** `[TESTIMONY]` `[CONTESTED]` Approximately four months before the
killings, the defendant contacted psychiatric providers reporting **intrusive
thoughts** and problems with her treatment plan.

**E-014** `[DOCUMENT]` In a December 2022 patient-portal message the defendant
asked to change an antidepressant, reporting she had begun experiencing intrusive
thoughts she had not previously had. The treating nurse practitioner responded
that intrusive thoughts are common in postpartum depression and anxiety, and
encouraged her to continue the medication longer.

**E-015** `[TESTIMONY]` The defendant messaged her provider nearly daily seeking
changes to her regimen, reporting side effects including insomnia, numbness, and
intrusive thoughts, and expressing concern about benzodiazepine dependence.

**E-016** `[TESTIMONY]` The defendant reported at various points either not
taking a full prescribed dose or wanting to reduce or switch prescriptions because
of side effects she found intolerable.

**E-017** `[TESTIMONY]` `[CONTESTED]` Care was fragmented. The treating nurse
practitioner testified she had never spoken with one of the defendant's
psychiatrists, had not accessed that psychiatrist's records, did not know how many
times the defendant had seen her, had not spoken to the out-of-state hospital that
admitted her, and did not have access to McLean Hospital's records. On
cross-examination the defense established that the defendant had seen that
psychiatrist approximately 14 times over the relevant four-month window.

**E-018** `[TESTIMONY]` `[THIN]` The defendant sought emergency care at least
once and was turned away from inpatient treatment. She also called a suicide
hotline during this period.

**E-019** `[TESTIMONY]` `[CONTESTED]` A treating provider testified she had
suggested the defendant might have a mood disorder or underlying bipolar disorder,
but did **not** appear to have postpartum psychosis.

**E-020** `[UNDISPUTED]` 2023-01-01 to 2023-01-05: the defendant was admitted as
an inpatient at McLean Hospital for five days. Her husband testified she was
frightened and did not want to go, and that he told her she had to.

**E-021** `[TESTIMONY]` Both the defendant and her husband later reported that
she received little active treatment at McLean and had not seen a physician for
several days of the admission.

**E-022** `[TESTIMONY]` `[EXTRA-RECORD]` Per the arrest warrant, the husband told
police the defendant had been having suicidal thoughts **and thoughts of harming
the children** in December 2022, and that she had reached out to her psychiatrist
and admitted herself to McLean as a result. *(Flag: it is unclear from reporting
in what form this reached the jury. This item is heavily load-bearing for both
theories — handle its provenance explicitly.)*

**E-023** `[TESTIMONY]` The husband described the defendant as a devoted mother
throughout. He testified he had felt hopeful after her hospital discharge, that
her mood seemed better and she was more active and able to participate in more
things.

## 4. DAY-OF TIMELINE — 2023-01-24

**E-030** `[TESTIMONY]` That morning the defendant took Cora to a medical
appointment. The husband photographed Dawson, who had dressed himself
independently for the first time.

**E-031** `[TESTIMONY]` During the day the defendant built a snowman in the yard
with the children and texted photos of them playing in the snow to her husband,
who was working in the basement. He testified she seemed to be in a good mood and
described it as one of her best days.

**E-032** `[DOCUMENT]` 16:53 — The defendant texted her husband asking about
takeout from a local restaurant (3V), saying she had not cooked and that it had
been a long day. He agreed; she asked him to check the menu.

**E-033** `[DOCUMENT]` `[CONTESTED]` In the hours before the killings the
defendant searched a CVS pharmacy's online inventory and then telephoned the store
regarding a product for her daughter.

**E-034** `[DOCUMENT]` `[CONTESTED]` The defendant conducted a search relating to
travel distance/time between the family home and the restaurant.

**E-035** `[TESTIMONY]` Shortly after 17:00 the husband left the house to collect
medication for Cora at the pharmacy and pick up the takeout order. Before leaving
he rocked the baby, saw his son eating on the couch, kissed his daughter on the
head, and said he would be right back.

**E-036** `[DOCUMENT]` The pharmacy is approximately a three-minute drive from
the home. Records show the husband sent an email before entering the store.

**E-037** `[DOCUMENT]` While at the pharmacy the husband called the defendant;
she did not answer. She returned the call roughly a minute later and they had a
brief conversation in which she confirmed a generic substitution was acceptable.
Investigators placed the killings at approximately this window.

**E-038** `[UNDISPUTED]` The husband was out of the house for approximately 25
minutes.

**E-039** `[TESTIMONY]` On returning, the husband found the house quiet, went
upstairs, found the bedroom door locked, and on entering saw blood and an open
window. He went outside and found the defendant on the ground. He called 911
reporting a suicide attempt.

**E-040** `[TESTIMONY]` After police arrived, the husband went to the basement
and found the three children, each with an exercise band around the neck. Cora
and Callan were in the main basement room; Dawson was in a separate office room.

**E-041** `[TESTIMONY]` `[THIN]` The defendant's self-inflicted wrist and neck
wounds were described as superficial and were not actively bleeding when found.

## 6. EXPERT TESTIMONY — DEFENSE

**E-060** `[TESTIMONY]` **Dr. Phillip Resnick**, forensic psychiatrist, specialist
in filicide, testified the defendant was clearly psychotic at the time of the
killings and should not be held criminally responsible.

**E-061** `[TESTIMONY]` Resnick characterized the voice the defendant reported as
an auditory command hallucination. He testified she experienced not only a command
but a sense that her body had been taken over by an external force, that she was
in a dream-like state, and that she lacked control over her own body — describing
her as like a puppet with someone else pulling the strings.

**E-062** `[CONTEXT]` Resnick testified for the defense in the Andrea Yates case;
Yates was found not guilty by reason of insanity at her 2006 retrial. *(Available
to the prosecution as a bias/consistency argument; available to the defense as
credentialing. Not itself evidence of this defendant's state.)*

**E-063** `[TESTIMONY]` `[THIN]` **Dr. Paul Zeizel**, forensic psychologist,
retained by the defense within days of the events and evaluating the defendant
continuously since.

**E-064** `[TESTIMONY]` `[THIN]` The defense called 10 witnesses in its case,
including psychiatric experts who testified the defendant suffered from postpartum
psychosis. Detail on the remainder is not available in reporting.

## 7. EXPERT TESTIMONY — PROSECUTION

**E-070** `[TESTIMONY]` **Dr. Kirk Heilbrun**, forensic psychologist, testified
he did not believe the defendant's account of hallucinating a voice directing her
to kill the children. He testified he believed instead that she took their lives
so they would not suffer after her own planned suicide.

**E-071** `[TESTIMONY]` Heilbrun testified the defendant had bipolar disorder and
depression, worsened by insomnia and adverse reactions to prescribed medications.
He nonetheless concluded she was criminally responsible, testifying she retained
an awareness of the illegality of killing others, including her children.

**E-072** `[CONTESTED]` The defense argued in closing that Heilbrun had conceded
the question could go either way, and characterized that as falling short of proof
beyond a reasonable doubt. The extent of any such concession is disputed.
`[ARGUMENT]`

**E-073** `[TESTIMONY]` **Dr. Avram Mack**, psychiatrist, interviewed the
defendant in April and testified in rebuttal to Resnick that she retained the
capacity to understand the seriousness of her actions.

**E-074** `[TESTIMONY]` **Dr. Gregory Saathoff**, forensic psychiatrist (FBI),
final witness, testified the defendant had the capacity to appreciate right from
wrong and to control her behavior. He pointed to preparatory planning: phone
searches, mapping the distance from the home to the restaurant, and acting within
the window when her husband was out. He testified he found it significant that the
killings occurred with no witness present, during a period in which she had
extended the errand of a potential witness.

**E-075** `[TESTIMONY]` On redirect, Saathoff confirmed testimony concerning the
defendant's calls to a suicide hotline. `[THIN]`

**E-076** `[PROCEDURAL]` During Heilbrun's testimony the defense moved for a
mistrial over remarks touching on the defendant's religion. Judge Sullivan denied
the motion, finding the witness rather than the prosecutor had raised it, but
admonished the prosecution and instructed jurors to disregard the remarks.

**E-077** `[TESTIMONY]` **Point of expert convergence:** psychiatric experts for
*both* sides characterized the killings as *altruistic filicide* — a parent
killing a child out of love, typically to spare the child anticipated suffering.
The dispute is over what that classification implies for capacity, not over the
classification.

## 9. COMPETING NARRATIVES — `[ARGUMENT]`, NOT EVIDENCE

> The two advocate agents should build their own cases from Sections 2–8. This
> section records what counsel actually argued so the simulation can be compared
> against the real trial. **Juror agents should not be given this section before
> closings.**

### 9.1 Commonwealth (ADA Jennifer Sprague; opening by ADA Shanan Buckingham)

- The defendant acted intentionally, rationally, and swiftly.
- The CVS inventory check, the call to the store, the added errand, and the
  restaurant-timing search together show she was constructing an uninterrupted
  window, deliberately extending her husband's absence.
- She was a functioning mother who knew right from wrong.
- The sequencing — moving Dawson to a separate room while Cora remained with
  Callan — reflects ordered, rational thinking.
- A person with no free will could not have executed the sequence of choices the
  killings required.
- The Commonwealth denied ever suggesting the defendant was coached to claim she
  heard voices, and stated no witness or evidence supported that characterization.

### 9.2 Defense (Kevin Reddington)

- Causation lies with the medical care: over-medication and inadequate,
  fragmented treatment transformed a healthy family into this outcome.
- The defendant was in the grip of postpartum psychosis and acted under a
  hallucinated command she could not resist.
- The investigation was inadequate, and prosecution experts distorted the
  evidence to minimize the depth of her illness.
- The prosecution's own experts failed to rebut Resnick and conceded uncertainty;
  uncertainty is not proof beyond a reasonable doubt.

## 11. SIMULATION CONTROLS

**S-001** Juror agents receive Sections 1–8 and 10. They do **not** receive
Section 0 (methodology), Section 9 (arguments) until closings, or this section.

**S-002** Juror agents render an initial sealed vote in isolation before any
deliberation content is shared.

**S-003** Evidence item order should be randomized per run within sections to
control for recency and primacy effects.

**S-004** The judge agent owns Section 1 and delivers C-004 and C-005 verbatim.
It rules on juror questions and determines when to deliver a Tuey-Rodriquez charge
and when to declare a hung jury.

**S-005** Verdict options presented: guilty of murder in the first degree; guilty
of murder in the second degree; guilty of manslaughter; not guilty by reason of
lack of criminal responsibility. Unanimity required.

**S-006** Minimum 30 runs before any inference is drawn. Report the distribution
and the hung-jury rate, not a verdict.

**S-007** Log every vote change with the juror's stated reason and the evidence
IDs cited. That log is the experimental output; the verdict is not.
